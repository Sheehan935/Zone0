# Defensible Space Overview

Current site model:

| Zone | Distance | Current Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 0 | 0-5 ft from structure | Ember-resistant zone. Non-combustible gravel, pavers, and hardscape only; no woody plants touching the structure. |
| Zone 1 | 5-30 ft | Lean, clean, and green. Low, well-spaced, well-irrigated native plants with no ladder fuels. |
| Zone 2 | 30-100 ft | Reduced fuel zone. Spaced shrubs, limbed-up trees, and regularly mowed grasses. |
| Zone 3 | 100+ ft | Wildland transition. Existing vegetation thinned and cleared of dead material, not newly planted. |

Core explanation:

The first 0-5 feet around the home is the most sensitive perimeter because
embers collect where wind pushes debris: wall corners, foundation edges,
stairwells, vents, deck joints, roof valleys, gutters, and mulch beds.

# Ember Dynamics

Current `/zone-0/` article draft:

> During a wildfire, it is rarely a giant wall of flame that burns down a
> house. It is the invisible blizzard of burning embers blowing miles ahead of
> the fire front.

Core point:

Embers behave like wind-driven snow. Wherever snow would drift and pile up
around a house, burning embers can also accumulate. Typical accumulation areas
include:

- corners
- stairwells
- foundation edges
- roof valleys
- gutters
- deck joists
- vents
- mulch beds
- piles of dry leaves
- objects stored against siding

# Non-Combustible Materials

Current `/materials/` article thesis:

> Replacing wood bark and organic mulch with fire-safe groundcovers is the
> single most effective upgrade you can make under AB 3074 regulations.

Core explanation:

When blowing embers land near a home, they need fuel to start a spot fire.
Inorganic hardscapes create a fuel break along the foundation line.

Recommended options:

- **Decomposed granite (DG):** Compacts into a solid, permeable surface and
  works well for walkways and perimeter borders.
- **Pea gravel and river rock:** Help drainage while creating an attractive
  ember break.
- **Concrete and stone pavers:** Create clean edges and structural hardscape
  for patios and paths adjacent to exterior walls.

Content note:

Avoid using "fireproof" as a claim. Prefer "non-combustible",
"ember-resistant", or "fuel break" depending on the sentence.

# 5-Step Zone 0 Inspection Checklist

Current homepage checklist:

## 1. Clear Organic Mulch

Remove bark, wood chips, pine needles, and organic matter within 5 feet of
walls.

Checklist note:

- non-combustible ground only

## 2. Install Gravel or DG

Replace organic mulch with crushed stone, river rock, gravel, or decomposed
granite.

Checklist note:

- minimum 2 inch rock depth

## 3. Prune and Thin Plants

Remove woody brush directly beneath windows and prune lower tree limbs under 6
feet.

Checklist note:

- eliminate fuel ladders

## 4. Clean Ember Traps

Clear dead leaves from gutters, roof valleys, and under balcony deck joists.

Checklist note:

- consider 1/8 inch metal mesh guards where appropriate

## 5. Relocate Combustibles

Move firewood piles, plastic furniture, and wooden planters out of the 0-5 foot
perimeter.

Checklist note:

- move combustibles 30+ feet away where practical

# FAQ Source

## What is AB 3074?

AB 3074 requires the Board of Forestry and Fire Protection to establish
regulations for an ember-resistant zone within 0 to 5 feet of structures in
Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones.

## Are bark and wood mulch allowed in Zone 0?

No. Organic mulches such as wood chips, bark, and pine needles ignite easily
when struck by windborne embers. Replace them with non-combustible materials
like gravel, decomposed granite, pavers, or bare earth.

## Do I have to remove all plants from the 5-foot perimeter?

Not necessarily. Low-growing, high-moisture plants may be acceptable in some
contexts when separated from the structure by non-combustible material and
maintained carefully, but local fire-authority enforcement controls the final
answer.

## Will non-compliance affect my home insurance?

Major insurance providers operating in California increasingly ask for proof of
defensible-space maintenance or risk-reduction steps before renewals or new
coverage in high-risk areas.
