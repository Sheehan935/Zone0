Zone 0 Homeowner Journey

Purpose

Evaluate the Zone 0 Landscaping website from the perspective of real homeowners.

The objective is to identify what makes a visitor:

STOP → UNDERSTAND → EXPLORE → RELATE IT TO THEIR PROPERTY → TAKE ACTION → RETURN → SHARE

The primary business action is the free Detailed Photo Review.

This skill is a behavioral UX evaluation. It is not primarily a visual design audit, code review, or SEO audit.

Do not change code during the evaluation.

⸻

1. PROJECT CONTEXT FIRST

Before evaluating the website:

1. Read CLAUDE.md.
2. Read PROJECT-TRUTH.md.
3. Inspect relevant project documentation in docs/ when necessary.
4. Determine the current site architecture and primary user flows.
5. Identify any locked decisions that affect the evaluation.
6. Treat production reality as authoritative where documented.

Respect the project’s existing operating principles.

Do not invent project requirements.

Do not override locked decisions.

If project documentation conflicts with an assumption in this skill, follow the current verified project documentation.

⸻

2. INSPECT THE REAL WEBSITE

Evaluate the actual website whenever possible.

Primary site:

https://zone0landscaping.com

Use available browser, preview, screenshot, or testing capabilities to experience the site as a user.

If browser interaction is available, use it.

If browser interaction is not available, inspect the live site and source files using the available tools, and clearly identify what could not be directly observed.

Do not pretend to have performed an interaction that was not actually performed.

The distinction is important:

OBSERVED
Something directly verified.

INFERRED
A reasonable conclusion based on observed evidence.

HYPOTHESIS
Something that should be tested with real users or analytics.

Never present an inference or hypothesis as an observed fact.

⸻

3. EVALUATION PRINCIPLE

Do not ask:

“Is this a good website?”

Ask:

“What would make this homeowner continue?”

And:

“What would make this homeowner care about their own property?”

And:

“What would make them want to upload a photo?”

The goal is not to maximize clicks.

The goal is to create useful momentum.

A successful experience should make the homeowner think:

“I came here to learn about Zone 0.”

Then:

“I wonder if my property has this problem.”

Then:

“What else should I check?”

Then:

“Maybe I should have them look at my property.”

⸻

4. REQUIRED PERSONAS

Evaluate the site as five different homeowners.

Do not collapse them into one generic user.

⸻

PERSONA 1 — CURIOUS HOMEOWNER

Situation

Has heard the term Zone 0 but does not know much about it.

Mindset

“I’ve heard people talking about this. I wonder if there’s anything I should be doing.”

Wants

Useful information without being overwhelmed.

Primary question

“Can this teach me something useful about my own property?”

⸻

PERSONA 2 — WILDFIRE-CONCERNED HOMEOWNER

Situation

Actively concerned about wildfire risk.

Mindset

“I need to know whether my property is prepared.”

Wants

Practical priorities, credible information, and clear next steps.

Primary question

“What should I fix first?”

Do not reward fear-based messaging.

Evaluate whether the site creates confidence and action without unnecessary alarm.

⸻

PERSONA 3 — SKEPTICAL HOMEOWNER

Situation

Does not automatically trust wildfire-related services.

Mindset

“Is this actually useful, or are you trying to sell me something?”

Wants

Evidence, credibility, transparency, and practical value.

Primary question

“Why should I trust you?”

Pay particular attention to claims, language, credentials, sales pressure, and unsupported assertions.

⸻

PERSONA 4 — INSTAGRAM VISITOR

Situation

Arrives from an Instagram post.

Mindset

“That post caught my attention. Let’s see what this is.”

Wants

Immediate relevance.

Behavior

Likely mobile and low-context.

Primary question

“Why should I stay?”

Evaluate whether the website successfully continues the promise or curiosity created by social content.

⸻

PERSONA 5 — ZONE 0 BEGINNER

Situation

Has never heard the term Zone 0.

Mindset

“What does that even mean?”

Wants

A simple explanation connected to their house and property.

Primary question

“What does this have to do with me?”

Do not assume terminology is understood.

⸻

6. REQUIRED QUESTIONS

Answer these questions for every persona.

A. What made me continue?

Identify the specific element that created momentum.

Examples:

* headline
* image
* question
* interaction
* useful information
* credibility
* curiosity
* relevance to the homeowner
* unexpected discovery

If nothing creates meaningful momentum:

No strong continuation trigger identified.

⸻

B. What confused me?

Identify:

* unclear terminology
* unclear claims
* confusing navigation
* competing CTAs
* excessive information
* missing context
* unclear hierarchy
* unexpected behavior
* unexplained concepts

Classify each as:

Minor friction

or

Journey-breaking confusion

⸻

C. What did I learn?

Answer:

“If I leave the website now, what do I know that I did not know before?”

Do not describe what the website says.

Describe what the homeowner actually learned.

⸻

D. What would I click next?

Choose the single most likely next action.

Do not simply list every available CTA.

Explain why that action would be most likely.

⸻

E. Why would I upload a photo?

This is critical.

Ask:

“Has the website given me enough reason to want someone to look at MY property?”

Identify the exact psychological or practical trigger.

If motivation is weak, explain what is missing.

⸻

F. Why would I leave?

Identify the most likely exit trigger.

Possible causes include:

* I learned enough.
* I am confused.
* I do not see personal relevance.
* It feels like a sales pitch.
* There is too much reading.
* I do not trust the claims.
* I cannot find what I came for.
* The experience becomes repetitive.
* There is no compelling next step.

Do not invent an exit reason without evidence.

⸻

G. Would I come back?

Answer:

YES

MAYBE

or

NO

Then explain why.

Identify what could create a legitimate reason to return.

Possible examples:

* check another area of the property
* investigate another plant
* compare before/after conditions
* continue learning
* check progress
* obtain another Photo Check
* share something useful with a neighbor
* revisit seasonal guidance

Do not recommend accounts, newsletters, or gamification merely to manufacture repeat visits.

⸻

7. FIND THE “ONE MORE THING” MOMENT

This is the most important part of the evaluation.

Find the point where the homeowner naturally thinks:

“Okay… what about this?”

Examples:

“Is this plant a problem?”

→ “What about the plant next to it?”

→ “What about my mulch?”

→ “What about the area around my house?”

→ “What should I fix first?”

→ “I should probably have someone look at a photo.”

Identify the strongest existing opportunity for this type of loop.

Explain:

1. What the homeowner just learned.
2. What natural question follows.
3. What could answer that question.
4. What the homeowner would likely do next.
5. How the loop could lead toward the free Detailed Photo Review.

⸻

8. IDENTIFY ENGAGEMENT LOOPS

Look specifically for these six loops.

Curiosity Loop

Something creates:

“What about my property?”

Discovery Loop

One useful discovery creates another question.

Personalization Loop

General information becomes personally relevant.

Photo Loop

The homeowner realizes:

“Someone should look at my property.”

Return Loop

There is a legitimate reason to return.

Sharing Loop

The homeowner thinks:

“I should send this to my spouse, neighbor, or friend.”

Identify which loops already exist.

Identify which are missing.

10. FIND THE COMMON PROBLEM

After evaluating all five personas, determine:

The single biggest stickiness problem

Ask:

“What is the most important reason a homeowner might leave without taking a meaningful action?”

Find the underlying behavioral problem.

Do not produce a list of unrelated problems here.

⸻

11. FIND THE COMMON STRENGTH

Determine:

“What is the strongest existing mechanism for keeping homeowners engaged?”

This may be:

* a specific section
* a specific interaction
* a specific message
* the Photo Check
* visual storytelling
* educational content
* credibility
* personalization

Protect this strength from unnecessary redesign.

12. RECOMMENDATIONS

Recommend no more than five improvements.

Prioritize by:

USER VALUE × BUSINESS IMPACT × IMPLEMENTATION EFFORT

For each recommendation provide:

Problem

What is currently limiting the journey?

Evidence

What did you actually observe?

Behavioral opportunity

What homeowner behavior could improve?

Proposed experience

What should happen?

Expected reaction

What should the homeowner think or do?

Photo Check connection

How does this naturally lead toward the free Detailed Photo Review?

Implementation complexity

LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH

Mobile considerations

How should it behave on a phone?

Measurement

How would we know it worked?

⸻

13. DO NOT OVERDESIGN

Avoid recommendations involving:

* unnecessary animation
* popups
* fake urgency
* fear-based messaging
* excessive CTAs
* badges
* points
* meaningless gamification
* forced accounts
* unnecessary personalization
* complicated dashboards
* intrusive email capture
* technical complexity without measurable value

The goal is not to make the website “busy.”

The goal is to make it useful enough that homeowners want to keep exploring.

⸻

14. ZONE 0 CONSTRAINTS

Respect current project decisions.

Do not:

* replace the site architecture without evidence
* introduce a framework
* introduce unnecessary dependencies
* remove Tailwind unless the project documentation or audit establishes a reason
* replace Tally
* add an address lookup tool
* create unnecessary accounts
* turn the website into a generic landscaping site
* expand the launch scope unnecessarily
* use fear as the primary conversion mechanism

The primary business action remains:

free Detailed Photo Review submission.

The Photo Check should feel like the logical next step after the homeowner discovers something relevant.

⸻

15. IMPLEMENTATION RULE

During the evaluation:

DO NOT MODIFY CODE.

First produce the analysis.

Then identify:

ONE improvement to implement first.

Explain why this improvement should come before the others.

Only modify code after explicit approval.

When implementation is eventually approved:

1. Read the relevant existing code.
2. Make the smallest appropriate change.
3. Preserve existing architecture.
4. Preserve locked decisions.
5. Test desktop.
6. Test mobile.
7. Test the complete journey.
8. Check for regressions.
9. Report exactly what changed.

SENIOR-LEVEL STANDARD

Be critical.

Do not flatter the website.

Do not invent problems to justify changes.

Do not recommend features because they are technically interesting.

Do not confuse visual polish with engagement.

Do not confuse clicks with value.

Protect things that already work.

Focus on homeowner psychology, clarity, curiosity, trust, usefulness, and momentum.

The ideal journey is:

“I came here to learn about Zone 0.”

→

“I understand what this means for my house.”

→

“I just learned something I didn’t know.”

→

“What about this other thing?”

→

“I should probably check my property.”

→

“Let me get the free Detailed Photo Review.”
