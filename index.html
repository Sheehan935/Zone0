Wildfire Ember Dynamics

Why the 5-Foot Zone Matters Most

Embers travel up to 2 miles ahead of a wildfire front. Over 90% of structure ignitions occur when blowing embers land in organic bark mulch or dry vegetation directly touching house siding.

90%

Perimeter Ignitions

Homes rarely ignite from radiant heat alone. They burn when wind-driven embers accumulate in organic bark chips, wood piles, or woody shrubs directly touching foundation walls.

0%

Organic Bark Chips

Cal Fire AB 3074 prohibits combustible wood mulch within 5 feet of structures. Replacing bark with decomposed granite or gravel creates an active ember break.

100%

Curb Appeal Preserved

Defensible space does not mean concrete. Flagstone walkways, crushed stone, and fire-adapted California succulents maintain a sophisticated aesthetic.

Regulatory Reference

Cal Fire AB 3074 Reference FAQ

What is Assembly Bill 3074?

Assembly Bill 3074 establishes a mandatory ember-resistant zone (Zone 0) within 0 to 5 feet of all structures located in State Responsibility Areas and High Fire Hazard Severity Zones across California.

Is wood bark mulch allowed within 5 feet of siding?

No. Organic wood chips, bark mulch, pine needles, and dry leaves are combustible and prohibited in Zone 0. They must be replaced with non-combustible materials like crushed stone, river rock, or decomposed granite.

Are all plants banned in Zone 0?

Not entirely, but high-fuel woody shrubs and resinous plants (such as Juniper or Rosemary) are strictly restricted. Low-growing, high-moisture plants (such as native succulents) separated by non-combustible stone borders are widely accepted.

Who conducts defensible space compliance inspections?

Local municipal fire departments (e.g., Berkeley Fire, Oakland Fire, Moraga-Orinda Fire District) and Cal Fire inspectors conduct periodic property inspections under California Public Resources Code § 4291.
