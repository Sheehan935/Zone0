Material Selection

Non-Combustible Materials for the 0–5 Foot Zone

Replacing wood bark and organic mulch with fire-safe groundcovers is the single most effective upgrade you can make under AB 3074 regulations.

When blowing embers land near your home, they require fuel to ignite a structure. Inorganic hardscapes act as a barrier, preventing ember embers from starting localized spot fires along your foundation line.

Recommended Groundcover Options

  • Decomposed Granite (DG): Compacts down into a solid, permeable surface perfect for walkways and zero-clearance perimeter borders.
  • Pea Gravel & River Rock: Excellent for natural drainage while creating an attractive, completely fireproof ember break.
  • Crushed Basalt: A denser, angular crushed stone that interlocks and resists scattering on slopes better than rounded gravel.
  • Concrete, Flagstone & Stone Pavers: Provides clean edges and structural hardscaping for patios directly adjacent to exterior walls.

Zone 0 Materials: Ember-Resistant vs. Combustible

A practical guide to materials and vegetation around the first 5 feet of your home. Requirements and applicability can vary by property and jurisdiction; verify current requirements with the appropriate official fire authority.

✅

EMBER-RESISTANT / GENERALLY PREFERRED

  • Decomposed Granite (DG) & Pea Gravel
  • River Rock & Crushed Basalt
  • Flagstone, Pavers, Concrete & Brick
  • Maintained Bare Soil
  • Non-Combustible Steel & Ceramic Succulent Planters
⚠️

COMBUSTIBLE MATERIALS TO AVOID / MANAGE

  • Wood Mulch & Bark Nuggets (“Gorilla Hair”)
  • Dry Leaves, Pine Needles & Uncleared Debris
  • Combustible fencing or attachments immediately adjacent to the structure
  • Combustible firewood & lumber storage under decks
  • Combustible vegetation or high-fuel shrubs immediately adjacent to the structure
